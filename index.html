        About the data

        Percentages count only streets named after individuals, not all streets. The 30-city figures come from Mapping Diversity (European Data Journalism Network, data extracted February 2023). In those cities, 52,888 streets honour people: 47,824 men, 4,779 women and 285 mixed, non-binary or uncertain. Gender comes from Wikidata, or from first names where Wikidata had none.

        The lines are drawn from EqualStreetNames (Open Knowledge Belgium, downloaded November 2026). Street names are shown exactly as they appear in OpenStreetMap, in the local language. Each street's namesake comes from its link to Wikidata; the person's name and description are Wikidata's English text. Streets whose namesake is not identified on Wikidata are left out. Streets named after several people are drawn in grey. Where a street name doesn’t spell out its namesake’s name, the card marks the link as unclear. Each city is drawn whole, as large as fits. EqualStreetNames maps fewer cities than Mapping Diversity, and some only in part, so its line counts differ from the 30-city figures.

        Source data, build scripts and full citations are in the project folder. This page collects nothing and loads nothing from outside its own files.
